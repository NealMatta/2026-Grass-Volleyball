# Rules

Adapted from the **Montrose Classic** (sand) for grass. Where we differ from Montrose, it's marked
**[GRASS]** — those changes exist because grass isn't sand.

---

## The vibe clause (read this one first)

**VIBES are everything. If your goal is to dominate every single game, this might not be the
tournament for you.** The goal is to have a good time, make new friends, and play some competitive
games.

Borrowed wholesale from Montrose, because they were right.

---

## Format

- **Coed 4s.** Four players on the court per team.
- **No gender requirement.** (Montrose requires a minimum number of women on court; we don't.)
- **You may play short-handed.** Three is fine. It's harder, but it's allowed.
- **Regular beach 4's rules apply**, with the extras below.

## Scoring

| | Points | Win by | Cap |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Pool games** | 21 | 2 | 23 |
| **Bracket games** | 25 | 2 | 27 |

- **Both scores get recorded, not just the winner.** Point differential is the third tiebreaker for
  bracket seeding, so a 21-8 win is worth more than a 21-19 win. Play the whole game out.
- Teams enter their score on the website right after the game ends. Paper sheets are at each court
  as a backup.
- Slots start 25 minutes apart. Keep an eye on the clock — eight slots on two courts is what fits
  the day.

## Ball handling

- **No open-hand tipping.** Hand must be closed, or use 2+ knuckles.
- **No set over.** If you push on the second hit, your shoulders must be square to the net.
- **Setting on serve is allowed.**
- **Players must rotate positions to serve.** No stacking one server.

## Timing

- Water break between every slot. It's August on the lakefront — actually drink it.

## [GRASS] What's different from sand

These are the adaptations. Sand rules assume things that aren't true on a field.

**No antennas** — Portable nets don't have them.
- The ball must cross **between the poles**.
- Hitting a **pole or a guy-line is out**.
- Hitting the **net cable between the poles is live** — play it.

**Boundaries** — Marked with cones or lines.
- **Ball touching the line is IN.**
- If there's no line and the call is genuinely disputed: **replay the point.** No arguing. This is
  a park, not a sanctioned match.

**Self-officiate the close stuff** — Call your own net touches, lifts, and foot faults. The assigned
referee team handles **score, rotation, and in/out only**. Everything else is on the honor system.

**Overhead obstruction** (tree, branch, wire) —
- Hits it **on the serve**: replay.
- Hits it **during a rally**: dead ball, point to the other team.

**Uneven or wet ground** — Play it where it lies. Any player may call an **unsafe stop** at any
time and the point is replayed. A rolled ankle ends someone's summer; a replayed point costs
30 seconds.

**Lets are live.** Serve clips the net and goes over: play it.

**Switching sides** — It's a lakefront site, so wind is likely. If there's a real wind or sun
advantage, **switch at 11** in pool games. If the field is neutral, skip it — the clock matters more.

---

## Refereeing

In every pool slot, **two teams are free and each referees one court**. Every team referees exactly
twice over the day, so the load is even and nobody gets stuck.

The referee team is responsible for:
- Keeping score out loud
- Watching the serve rotation
- In/out calls

Not responsible for: net touches, lifts, doubles. Those are self-called.

---

## Tiebreakers

Used to seed the bracket after pool play, applied in order:

1. **Wins**
2. **Head-to-head** (if the tied teams played each other)
3. **Point differential**
4. **Points scored**
5. **Rock paper scissors** — captains, in front of everyone

The website computes 1–4 automatically. If two teams are still dead level after all four, the site
will say so and Neal settles it with #5.
